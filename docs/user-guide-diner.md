# Booking a table: guide for diners

This guide explains what you see on the SeatPlan booking page of a restaurant
and what happens behind each step. All times are the restaurant's local time.

## 1. Choose a date and your party size

The page lists every seating time of that date, grouped by service (lunch,
dinner). Each time has one of three labels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Available | Several tables can seat your party for the whole stay |
| Limited | Only one or two tables are left, or the kitchen is almost at its limit for that time |
| Full | Nothing fits: the time has passed, every suitable table is taken, or too many guests already arrive at that time |

Why can a time be full while tables look free? The restaurant limits how many
guests may *arrive* at each seating time, so the kitchen is not asked to start
too many meals at once. That limit is the "covers cap".

Closed dates (holidays, private events) show the reason instead of times.

The party sizes on offer stop at the biggest party the restaurant can really
seat online: its largest table or tables that may be joined, and never more
than the covers cap of a seating time. For a bigger group, please call the
restaurant.

Tell us if you need an **accessible table** or **high chairs**: only tables
that offer them are then shown as available.

## 2. Pick a time and look at the map

The map shows every table for your stay (your time plus the usual time a party
of your size stays, for example 90 minutes for two people):

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Free | You can take it |
| Held | Another diner is choosing it right now (for at most 5 minutes) |
| Taken | Already booked for part of your stay |
| Too small | Seats fewer people than your party |
| Blocked | Not suitable: kept for bigger parties, or missing the accessibility or high-chair room you asked for |

SeatPlan also **recommends** a table and says why, for example "Exact fit for
2" or "Keeps the table for 6 free for a larger party". A large party may be
offered two or three tables joined together.

## 3. Hold, then confirm

When you pick a table (or let SeatPlan pick for you), it is **held for you
for 5 minutes** while you type your name and email. Nobody else can take it
during that time. If the time runs out, pick the table again. If you go
back and choose another table, the first one is released straight away.

To keep tables free for everyone, one person can hold at most 3 tables per
date at a time, and holds can never cover more than half the tables of the
restaurant at once. If you see "Many tables are being chosen right now",
try again a few minutes later.

If someone else was faster, you get a friendly message with the nearest free
times and the other free tables, so you can choose again straight away.

## 4. Your confirmation email and manage link

After confirming you receive an email with a **personal manage link**. Keep it
private: anyone with the link can change or cancel the booking. We store only
a scrambled (hashed) copy of the link, so even we cannot read it back.

You also receive a reminder the day before (or two hours before, if you booked
at short notice). If you change the booking, the old reminder is cancelled
and a new one follows the new time.

## 5. Change or cancel

Open your manage link to:

- **change the time or date**: if your table is still free you keep it,
  otherwise SeatPlan finds the best free table for the new time;
- **change the party size**: if your table no longer fits, you are moved to
  one that does (you will see the new table straight away);
- **cancel**: the table is released at once and you receive a confirmation.

Every change sends you an email with the new details and an updated calendar
invite. Changes are possible until the booking starts.

## 6. The waitlist

If your time is full, join the waitlist. You see your **place in line** and an
**estimated wait**. The estimate works like this:

1. We count the tables (or allowed combinations) that could seat your party: k.
2. Each of them frees up about once per "turn time", the time a party of that
   table's size usually stays. We average those turn times: T minutes.
3. With k tables each freeing every T minutes, one frees up about every T / k
   minutes. If you are number p in line, you wait about **p x T / k** minutes,
   rounded up to the next 5 minutes.

Example: four tables fit a couple, they turn every 90 minutes, and you are
second: 2 x 90 / 4 = 45 minutes.

It is an estimate, not a promise. When a booking that suits you is cancelled,
the first matching person on the waitlist receives an email with a link to
book that table. "Matching" means the cancelled booking freed a table at the
time you asked for, the table fits your party, and your time has not passed
yet: you are never offered a table for a time that is already gone.
